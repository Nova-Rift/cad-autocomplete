The system design

Top row: what happens each time you draw. Bottom row: how the model was made.

How this was made

1

Free drawings

9.2 million sketches

Real sketches people drew in a design tool, collected by Princeton researchers (SketchGraphs). Each one lists its shapes in the order they were drawn.

No hand labelling: the shape a person drew next is the right answer.

2

Drawings become numbers

line 12 16 52 16

A model only reads numbers. Each shape becomes its kind (line, circle, arc, point) plus its positions on a 64 × 64 grid. A sketch becomes a sentence, and the job becomes guessing the next word.

3

Cleaning

9.2 → 2.5 million

Most sketches were copies of each other, so only one of each was kept. Over a third of the test sketches also sat in the training pile and were taken out of the test.

Why: a test the model has already seen flatters the score.

4

The model

4.8 million weights

A small model of the same family as chat AIs, written from scratch. It reads the shapes so far and scores every possible next number.

5

Training

72 minutes · $1.64

On one rented GPU the model guesses, gets corrected, and its weights are nudged, 60,000 times. Weight decay keeps the weights small, and the copy that does best on unseen sketches is the one kept.

Watched throughout: the gap between seen and unseen sketches, the sign of memorising.

6

Scoring

43% exactly right

Tested on 5,000 sketches it never saw, hiding the next shape 31,859 times. The top guess is exactly the shape the person drew 43% of the time, the right shape is in the top three 54% of the time, and it is the right kind of shape 74% of the time.

Showing only confident guesses: when the model is at least 50% sure, 77% of its guesses are exactly right (precision), and it speaks up on 39% of steps.

7

Into the browser

0.13 seconds a guess

The model was rewritten in plain JavaScript so it runs on this page with no server. It was checked against the GPU version and gives the same guesses.

⚙

The settings used

Learning rate 0.001 (eased in over the first 1,000 steps, then slowly lowered) · 1,024 sketches per step · weight decay 0.05 · no dropout · 60,000 steps, about 24 passes over the data · 6 layers, 256 wide, 4 heads.

Note: these were first picks and nothing has been tuned. Both error lines were still falling when training stopped, so a longer run or a bigger model should score higher.

!

What did not work at first

Guesses ran to the page edge. The training sketches were all stretched to fill the page, and the first model learned to expect that. On sketches that do not fill the page its top guess was right only 26% of the time. Training was restarted with sketches shrunk and shifted at random: 31% after the same amount of training, 34% when finished.

Too many helpers. The cleaning step ran 96 helper programs on a machine with about 18 processors. They tripped over each other and it looked frozen. With 16 it took two minutes.

A silent wait. That first run printed nothing, so there was no telling stuck from slow. It was restarted with progress messages.

Missing tools. The rented machine lacked the file-copy tool and parts of the data reader; each was installed by hand.

Since then: every long job reports its progress.

Results

Not done yet

It guesses shapes only, not the rules between them (parallel, equal, mirrored), which the data also holds. It handles up to 16 shapes on a 64 × 64 grid. It does not yet learn from which guesses people take.
